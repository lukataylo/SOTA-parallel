# Local flood history (Blackfriars Road / Southwark station) and London extreme short-storm rainfall

Reading notes: "Verified" means I read the source document or page itself. "Snippet only" means the figure came from a search-result summary of a page I could not open (Thames Water and London Fire Brigade pages returned 404 or empty files; londonfloodreview.co.uk is now a parked domain). Treat snippet-only figures as unverified.

## 1. Recorded flooding on Blackfriars Road, at or near Southwark station, The Cut, Union Street, Boundary Row

### Takeaway
Flooding is documented at this exact location, but the only large event was a burst 36-inch water main on 21 January 2020 (not rainfall); the rainfall-related records are small nuisance incidents (10 May 2006 footway ponding at Blackfriars Road / Boundary Row; a London Fire Brigade flood call at the Old Vic on The Cut during the 23 June 2016 storm). No published water depth exists for any of them, and I found no record of rainfall flooding here on 12 or 25 July 2021.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- For a rainfall-driven model, the 2020 event is not a validation case: it was a pressurised trunk main failure. It does show where water pooled and that the station entrance and crossroads are a low point that closes the station when flooded.
- The rainfall-related evidence here is weak nuisance flooding (kerb ponding, blocked drains, one fire brigade call in a roughly 44 mm storm day). The model should not predict deep, widespread flooding here for storms in the 20 to 45 mm range without the record contradicting it; shallow ponding at kerbs and low points is consistent with the record.
- "Blackfriars Road 2006 to 2020" in the SWMP almost certainly counts the 2020 burst main as a "surface water incident", so the SWMP cluster overstates rainfall flooding.
- Devon Mansions is on Tooley Street, about 2 km east of the site, and is not relevant to the 512 m square in either version.

### Gaps
- No water depth was published for any incident at this location (2006, 2007, 2016 or 2020).
- No source found for Nicholson Street or Great Suffolk Street flooding. "Great Southwark Street" in the SWMP may mean Great Suffolk Street, but that is a guess.
- The London Fire Brigade page for January 2020 could not be opened; the area and property counts conflict between snippets.
- No evidence found either way on whether this location flooded on 12 or 25 July 2021. Southwark is not among the boroughs named as worst hit on 12 July (Camden, Hammersmith and Fulham, Westminster, Kensington and Chelsea).

## 2. Southwark flood risk documents: Critical Drainage Area status, recorded flooding, sewer capacity

### Takeaway
The site is not in any of Southwark's five Critical Drainage Areas (2011 plan), and the 2022 plan dropped that designation in favour of catchments and "hotspots"; the site sits in Catchment S01 Waterloo, with no hotspot on Blackfriars Road itself but several on side streets within a few hundred metres. The documents say the combined sewer network is old, at capacity in places, and not built for a 1 in 100 year storm; no document states an original design standard for this area.

### Cited Findings
- The 2011 plan identified five Critical Drainage Areas: "Herne Hill, Central Southwark, East Southwark, Camberwell and London Bridge". The northern one is "a small pocket in the north, in the vicinity of London Bridge Rail Station". — [Southwark SFRA, sections 2.4.1 and 3.3.2](https://www.southwark.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-05/southwark_council_sfra.pdf)
- The 2022 plan moves "away from previous Critical Drainage Areas (CDAs) to identify Hotspot areas within new" catchments. Catchment S01 Waterloo "previously contained a small section of CDA Group7_035, in the top right-hand corner of the Catchment covering Southwark Cathedral and the London Bridge station area". Southwark underground station is listed as infrastructure inside Catchment S01. No flood alleviation schemes are ongoing or completed in this catchment. — [Southwark SWMP v2.0, sections 4.1 to 4.2](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- Catchment S01 risk description: "Areas at risk from a 1 in 30-year rainfall event are particularly prevalent within highways in the northern half of the Catchment. Although the RoFSW does not seem to form flow paths in this Catchment, there are areas of potential pooling to the south of the Tate Modern art gallery, and between Blackfriars Road and the York Road / Waterloo Road roundabout". — [Southwark SWMP v2.0, section 4.3](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- Properties at risk in Catchment S01 (Environment Agency surface water mapping): 161 in the 1 in 30 year event, 591 in the 1 in 100 year event, 2,083 in the 1 in 1000 year event. — [Southwark SWMP v2.0, Table 4-1](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- Hotspots in S01 with properties at risk in the 1 in 100 year event: Copperfield Street (52); Emerson Street and Park Street (36); Southwark Bridge Road, Sudrey Street, Bittern Street (28); Colombo Street and Meymott Street (26); Clink Street (23); Burrows Mews (17); Rushworth Street (17); Rushworth Street, King's Bench Street and Pocock Street (16). Blackfriars Road, The Cut and Union Street are not named as hotspots. — [Southwark SWMP v2.0, Table 4-2](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- Topography: Basin A "slopes from south to north; water will flow towards the River Thames". — [Southwark SWMP v2.0, section 3.4.1](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- Sewers: "The sewerage network in the area is made up mostly of combined sewers ... The sewer system and associated infrastructure is dated and not built to withstand high intensity rainfall such as a 1 in 100-year event." — [Southwark SWMP v2.0, section 2.6](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- Thames Water sewer flood incident records by postcode sector: SE1 0 has 1 incident (internal, in the "1 in 20 year" column); SE1 9 has 1 (external); SE1 2 has 3; borough total 132, concentrated in the south (SE22 0 has 37). "at least one internal flood incident has occurred in SE1 0 which is fully contained by the Catchment". — [Southwark SWMP v2.0, Table 2-2 and section 4.3](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- The Local Flood Risk Management Strategy 2023 to 2029 describes "Thames Water's historic combined sewer system which is at capacity" (said of the London Bridge area), and gives borough totals of 2,365 properties at surface water risk in a 1 in 30 year event, 7,130 in 1 in 100, 23,880 in 1 in 1000. — [Southwark LFRMS 2023-2029](https://www.southwark.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2025-04/Southwark%20Local%20Flood%20Risk%20Management%20Strategy%202023%20to%202029.pdf)
- London Flood Review Stage 4: "Both rainfall events exceeded the current, and expected design capacity of the below-ground system"; in Kensington and Chelsea "the sewer system was surcharged because it was not designed to cope with the flow"; in Waltham Forest "the gully capacity was such that flows could not get into the sewer system" while the sewer still had capacity. Thames Water local schemes are designed to a 1 in 30 year storm (for example Tamplin Mews, "designed to protect 120 properties up to a 1-in-30 year storm"). — [London Flood Review Stage 4 technical report](https://www.thameswater.co.uk/media-library/zwka4vhh/london-flood-review-stage-4-technical.pdf)
- Borough-wide notable rainfall floods on record: 27 April 2004 (Dulwich and Herne Hill), 14 June 2006, 20 July 2007, 14 August 2014 (Peckham), 23 June 2016. All the serious ones are in the south and centre of the borough, not the north. — [Southwark SFRA, section 3.2.2](https://www.southwark.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-05/southwark_council_sfra.pdf)

### Inferences
- The council's own view is that this part of north Southwark has scattered ponding on highways rather than a connected flow path, which matches flat terrain close to the Thames. A model showing shallow, patchy ponding in road low points is consistent; a model showing a deep continuous flood route down Blackfriars Road would not match the council mapping.
- Hotspots at Burrows Mews, Colombo Street / Meymott Street, Copperfield Street and Pocock Street / King's Bench Street are probably inside or on the edge of the 512 m square (my estimate from street locations, not from the document's maps). They are useful spot checks.
- A 1 in 30 year capacity for the combined sewers is a reasonable working assumption for new and upgraded Thames Water assets, but the documents do not say the Victorian sewers under Blackfriars Road meet it. Gully inlet capacity may be the binding limit in intense bursts.

### Gaps
- I found no Section 19 flood investigation report by Southwark covering this area.
- The hotspot and incident maps are image appendices; I could not read exact positions relative to the square.
- No document gives a stated design return period for the existing combined sewers in SE1.

## 4. Rainfall of the 12 July 2021 and 25 July 2021 London storms

### Takeaway
On 12 July 2021 the worst-hit point (Bayswater / Paddington) received more than 90 mm in the day with peak intensities above 100 mm/h, estimated at around or above 1 in 200 years; on 25 July the worst point (Walthamstow area) received more than 80 mm with peak intensities around 50 to 100 mm/h. Official gauges mostly missed the cores, recording about 40 to 48 mm, so the high figures come from radar, and I could not open the review's stage 1 to 3 reports to get 5, 15, 30 and 60 minute gauge maxima.

### Cited Findings

London Flood Review (independent review commissioned by Thames Water, 2022)
- Thames Water planned for 20 to 30 mm on both dates. On 12 July "significant areas received rainfall of over 50mm, with the most affected place seeing more than 90mm". On 25 July "rainfall of over 50mm was even more widespread than on 12 July, with the worst affected point seeing over 80mm". Average July rainfall in London is about 45 mm. Over 1,500 properties reported flooding. (Verified) — [London Flood Review Stage 4 technical report](https://www.thameswater.co.uk/media-library/zwka4vhh/london-flood-review-stage-4-technical.pdf)
- 12 July: most intense rain over Bayswater; "rainfall intensities exceeded 100mm/hr ... detailed rainfall analysis indicates the event could be greater than a 1 in 400-year return period in some locations". Peak intensity coincided with the tidal peak, so sewer outfalls were tide-locked. 25 July: most intense rain over Walthamstow; "rainfall intensities peaked at around 50mm/hr", lower than 12 July but over a much wider area, again at high tide. Described overall as "2 months of rain fell in 2 hours". (Verified; paper by a review team member) — [Barbour, CIWEM paper on the London Flood Review](https://www.ciwem.org/assets/pdf/assets/uploads/Fiona_Barbour_paper.pdf)
- Snippet only (Stage 3 summary): 69 km2 of the 1,292 km2 catchment exceeded a 1 in 30 year rainfall return period on 12 July, and 123 km2 did on 25 July; over a small area of Bayswater the 12 July storm exceeded 1 in 200 years. — [London Flood Review Stage 3](https://www.thameswater.co.uk/media-library/home/about-us/investing-in-our-region/flooding-review/london-flood-review-stage-3.pdf)

Westminster Section 19 report (uses the review's radar analysis and Met Office gauge data; verified)
- 12 July: Putney Heath Reservoir gauge recorded 40.1 mm between 14:00 and 17:00 and 47.4 mm in 24 hours; Kew Gardens recorded 40.2 mm between 15:00 and 18:00. St James's Park and Holland Park "did not record a significant amount of rainfall" on 12 July. The report says the gauges likely missed the peak. — [Westminster Section 19 report](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/documents/Section%2019%20(Final%20V1.4).pdf)
- 12 July radar: Westminster "received a maximum rainfall intensity of over 150 mm/hr, with the majority of the Borough receiving a maximum rainfall intensity of between 50–100 mm/hr". Highest return period in Paddington, "just under 1 in 200 years"; the 2-hour-duration return period map shows parts above 1 in 200. — [Westminster Section 19 report](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/documents/Section%2019%20(Final%20V1.4).pdf)
- 25 July: Holland Park gauge recorded about 18.6 mm between 13:30 and 17:00 (20.6 mm in 24 hours); St James's Park recorded about 41.8 mm on the day. Radar maximum intensity in Westminster 50 to 100 mm/h; most of Westminster was a 1 in 5 year event, worst areas 1 in 10. — [Westminster Section 19 report](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/documents/Section%2019%20(Final%20V1.4).pdf)

Other sources
- Kew, 12 July: 47.8 mm, reported by JBA (citing the Independent) as "in a single hour", and as Kew's wettest day since 6 July 1983. St James's Park 25 July: 41.8 mm in the day; JBA's own estimate of that daily total is roughly 1 in 50 to 1 in 100 years, based on gridded reanalysis, not gauge statistics. Pudding Mill Lane station had flood water 0.4 to 0.5 m deep on 25 July. — [JBA Risk Management retrospective](https://www.jbarisk.com/knowledge-hub/event-response/a-retrospective-look-at-summer-2021-london-flash-floods/)
- Conflict: the Westminster report's hourly gauge data gives Kew 40.2 mm over three hours on 12 July, so "47.8 mm in a single hour" is probably the daily total misreported as hourly. — [Westminster Section 19 report](https://www.westminster.gov.uk/sites/default/files/media/documents/Section%2019%20(Final%20V1.4).pdf); contradicted by [JBA](https://www.jbarisk.com/knowledge-hub/event-response/a-retrospective-look-at-summer-2021-london-flash-floods/)
- Met Office monthly summary: parts of London recorded "more than double the average rainfall they'd expect in the month" in July 2021; no station totals given. — [Met Office blog, August 2021](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/blog/2021/heatwave-helps-mark-fifth-warmest-july-on-record)
- Snippet only: "nearly 76 mm of rain fell in 90 minutes in parts of London on 12 July". — [Milliman](https://www.milliman.com/en-GB/insight/UK-2021-extreme-weather-events)

Earlier London and Southwark short storms (for comparison; verified)
- 27 April 2004, Dulwich / Herne Hill: "A reported 60 mm of rain fell in just under one hour"; roads "became rivers deeper than 300mm"; about 200 properties flooded. The 2022 plan instead says "60mm of rainfall fell over two hours". — [Southwark SFRA Appendix E](https://www.southwark.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-06/appendix_e_-_historic_flooding_rev3.pdf); contradicted on duration by [Southwark SWMP v2.0, section 14.3](https://services.southwark.gov.uk/assets/attach/280890/Southwark_SWMP_v2.0.pdf)
- 14 June 2006: "Heavy rainfall (40mm in an hour) caused surface water flooding in some parts of the Borough and the closure of Rotherhithe Station". — [Southwark SFRA Appendix E](https://www.southwark.gov.uk/sites/default/files/2024-06/appendix_e_-_historic_flooding_rev3.pdf)

### Inferences
- Realistic "severe but observed" London storm for the model: 40 to 60 mm in about an hour (2004, 2006, gauge totals in July 2021). Realistic "extreme, at the storm core" case: 80 to 90 mm over roughly 1 to 3 hours with 15-minute bursts at 100 to 150 mm/h (25 to 38 mm in 15 minutes). The centre of London's worst recent storm was about 3 km north-west of the site, not over it.
- Central London gauges near the site (St James's Park, about 2 km west) recorded little on 12 July and 41.8 mm on 25 July, so the site itself probably saw a moderate event on 25 July and a minor one on 12 July. This fits the absence of flood reports at Southwark station in July 2021.

### Gaps
- No verified 5, 15, 30 or 60 minute maxima at named gauges (Shepherd's Bush, Maida Vale, Walthamstow, Stratford). These are in London Flood Review Stage 1 and Stage 2, which I could not open (Thames Water links returned empty files; the review's own website is defunct).
- No Met Office return period statement for either storm was found.
- Storm durations are not stated precisely; sources say "about 2 hours" for the main burst, with gauge accumulation over about 3 hours.

## 5. UK records and design values for short-duration rainfall; climate change uplifts

### Takeaway
UK records are 92 mm in 60 minutes and 80 mm in 30 minutes, so the upper part of the team's test range (anything near 120 mm in 15 to 60 minutes, or above about 80 mm in 30 minutes) is beyond anything recorded in the UK. Environment Agency uplifts for London are 20 to 40% on peak rainfall. I did not find Flood Estimation Handbook depth tables for central London.

### Cited Findings
- Met Office UK records: 5 minutes, about 32 mm (Preston, 10 August 1893); 30 minutes, 80 mm (Eskdalemuir, 26 June 1953); 60 minutes, 92 mm (Maidenhead, 12 July 1901); 90 minutes, 117 mm (Dunsop Valley, 8 August 1967); 120 minutes, 193 mm (Walshaw Dean Lodge, 19 May 1989, with reservations) or 155 mm (Hewenden Reservoir, 11 June 1956); 155 minutes, 169 mm (Hampstead, Greater London, 14 August 1975); 180 minutes, 178 mm (Horncastle, 7 October 1960). The Met Office table has no 15-minute record. — [Met Office UK climate extremes](https://www.metoffice.gov.uk/research/climate/maps-and-data/uk-climate-extremes)
- Environment Agency peak rainfall allowances, London management catchment (1981 to 2000 baseline). For the 1 in 30 year (3.3%) event: 2050s epoch (2022 to 2060) central 20%, upper end 35%; 2070s epoch (2061 to 2125) central 20%, upper end 35%. For the 1 in 100 year (1%) event: 2050s central 20%, upper end 40%; 2070s central 25%, upper end 40%. — [Environment Agency allowances table](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/623c84c9e90e0779a4d558a1/Peak_rainfall_climate_change_allowances_by_management_catchment.ods); [guidance page](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/peak-rainfall-climate-change-allowances-by-management-catchment)
- One SE1 drainage design example (Vinegar Yard, near London Bridge, about 1.5 km east of the site) uses peak rainfall intensities of 32.4 mm/h for the 1 in 1 year storm and 103.2 mm/h for the 1 in 100 year storm in a Modified Rational Method calculation, then adds 40% for climate change. The storm duration behind these intensities is not stated (such calculations normally use a short critical duration of a few minutes to 15 minutes). — [Vinegar Yard Drainage Assessment Report](https://data.london.gov.uk/download/vql0l/28b96b8e-c46b-4031-b790-c6d063a39893/18.%2020211029-4188-Vinegar%20Yard%20Drainage%20Assessment%20Report.pdf)
- A UK government research report lists "extreme rainfall" recognition thresholds of 45 mm in 15 minutes, 62 mm in 30 minutes and 79 mm in 60 minutes (snippet only; these are national event-recognition thresholds, not London design depths). — [Extreme Rainfall and Flood Event Recognition technical report](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/60266c05d3bf7f031a9e59e5/Extreme_Rainfall___Flood_Event_Recognition_technical_report.pdf)
- Snippet only: SE1 and Camden drainage calculations using the older Flood Studies Report method take the 5-year, 60-minute depth as about 20 to 21 mm. — [Camden planning document](https://camdocs.camden.gov.uk/CMWebDrawer/Record/9283750/file/document)

### Inferences
- Banding for the 10 to 120 mm, 15 to 60 minute test range, for London:
  - 10 to 25 mm in 60 minutes: frequent (a few years' return period or less; the 5-year hourly depth is about 20 mm).
  - 40 to 60 mm in 60 minutes: rare but observed in London (2004, 2006, July 2021 gauges); roughly the 1 in 30 to beyond 1 in 100 year range. The exact return period depends on Flood Estimation Handbook data I did not obtain.
  - 80 to 92 mm in 60 minutes: at or near the UK record; comparable to the radar-estimated core of 12 July 2021 (over a longer period). A plausible "worst credible" case, especially with a 40% uplift on a 1 in 100 year depth.
  - Above 92 mm in 60 minutes, above 80 mm in 30 minutes, or above about 45 to 55 mm in 15 minutes: exceeds UK records. Useful as stress tests only, not as realism checks.
- Applying the 40% upper-end uplift to a present-day 1 in 100 year hourly depth of roughly 50 to 60 mm (my rough estimate, unverified) gives about 70 to 85 mm, so about 85 to 90 mm in an hour is a sensible ceiling for "realistic with climate change".

### Gaps
- Flood Estimation Handbook (FEH13 or FEH22) depth-duration-frequency values for central London (1 in 30 and 1 in 100 year depths for 15, 30 and 60 minutes) were not found in any open source; they are licensed data from the UK Centre for Ecology and Hydrology web service. The return period bands above are therefore approximate.
- No verified UK 15-minute record. A figure of 56 mm in 15 minutes (Bolton, 1893) is sometimes quoted, but I could not confirm it and it is not in the Met Office table.
